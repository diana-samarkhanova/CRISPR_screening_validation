# Competitor audit for CRISPR-EvidenceRank

**Audit date:** 30 July 2026  
**Scope:** academic and openly documented methods that call, correct, predict, rank, annotate, or validate pooled CRISPR-screen hits, with emphasis on drug-response screens. Only primary papers, preprints, and official project/database pages were used. This is a targeted landscape audit, not proof that no unpublished or proprietary system exists.

## Executive conclusion

Several systems overlap with parts of the proposed product, but they solve different prediction problems:

1. **SkywalkR** is the closest post-screen precedent. It re-ranked thousands of KO/CRISPRa resistance hits using screen, clinical, literature, knowledge-graph, DepMap, and tractability evidence, then prospectively tested selected genes. Its central ranking is an unsupervised, multi-objective recommender built for an EGFR-inhibitor/NSCLC dataset; it was not trained or calibrated against a cross-study registry of orthogonal validation successes and failures.
2. **VirtualCRISPR** predicts whether a `perturbation method × gene × cell line × phenotype` tuple will be called a hit in a CRISPR screen. It predicts author-defined screen outcomes before an experiment, not whether an observed guide-level hit will reproduce in an independent assay. It does not consume counts, guide effects, replicates, copy number, or screen-quality measurements.
3. **AssayBench** is a large adjacent pre-screen benchmark. It asks a model to predict a CRISPR-screen gene ranking from an assay description across heterogeneous phenotype classes. Its target is the author-method screen result, not whether an already observed hit will validate independently.
4. **PitViper, MAGeCK, drugZ, Chronos, CRISPRcleanR, CERES, and related methods** provide screen processing, statistical hit calling, consensus analysis, or artifact correction. They estimate evidence within the screen; they do not learn the probability of later orthogonal reproduction from validation-event labels.
5. **Project Score, GEMap, PATH, TargetPro/TargetBench, and GETgene-AI** prioritize therapeutic targets using dependency, multi-omic, network, literature, druggability, or clinical-stage evidence. Their target labels and outputs are not CRISPR-screen validation outcomes.
6. **BioGRID ORCS, iCSDB, and FITdb** are important evidence sources or comparison interfaces, but they do not provide a curated `tested → reproduced / failed / discordant` validation-event registry.

The defensible niche is therefore **post-screen prediction of orthogonal reproducibility**, not generic “AI ranking of CRISPR genes.”

## Scope exclusions that should not be marketed as direct competitors

Single-cell perturbation foundation models and “virtual cell” models generally predict post-perturbation expression, not whether a pooled CRISPR hit will reproduce in an independent assay. A 2025 benchmark found that several deep models did not consistently outperform simple linear baselines for unseen-perturbation expression prediction: Ahlmann-Eltze et al., “Deep-learning-based gene perturbation effect prediction does not yet outperform simple linear baselines,” *Nature Methods* 22, 1657–1661 (2025), [doi:10.1038/s41592-025-02772-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41592-025-02772-6). These models may later supply expression-response priors, but they should not be used as evidence that orthogonal validation success has already been solved.

## Defensible novelty statement

The strongest claim supported by this audit is:

> **As of 30 July 2026, this targeted audit found no public academic framework that jointly (i) ingests a user’s actual guide- or count-level drug-response CRISPR screen, (ii) quantifies guide, replicate, initial-library/pDNA, copy-number and proximity artifacts, (iii) integrates drug, cell-line and disease-context evidence, and (iv) is trained and evaluated against structured independent orthogonal-validation outcomes under study-, drug-, cell-line- and temporal holdouts to estimate a calibrated hit-reproducibility score.**

Recommended manuscript positioning:

> **CRISPR-EvidenceRank is an artifact-aware, context-specific framework for predicting the orthogonal reproducibility of drug-response CRISPR-screen hits.**

This wording is narrower but substantially more defensible than “the first AI system for CRISPR-hit prioritization.”

### Claims to avoid

- “First AI/ML model to rank CRISPR-screen hits.” SkywalkR and VirtualCRISPR make this false.
- “First platform to integrate CRISPR with multi-omics, knowledge graphs, or druggability.” Project Score, SkywalkR, GEMap, PATH, TargetPro, and GETgene-AI already cover combinations of these.
- “Predicts experimental validation probability” before explicit successes **and** attempted failures are collected and calibration is demonstrated.
- “MAGeCK-significant non-hits are negatives.” Most unselected genes were never independently tested.
- “CNV-aware” based only on importing a corrected gene score. The report should expose the raw-to-corrected rank/effect change and the assumptions of the correction method.
- “Universal across CRISPR-KO, CRISPRi, and CRISPRa” if the initial model is trained mainly on nuclease KO screens. CNV cutting artifacts and effect directions are modality-specific.

Until the label registry contains enough explicit failed/discordant tests, the principal output should be called a **relative reproducibility score**, not an absolute probability.

## Consequences for model and benchmark design

### Keep four outputs separate

The literature shows that these are distinct estimands:

1. **Screen evidence:** statistical credibility of the measured phenotype.
2. **Orthogonal reproducibility:** probability/rank of reproducing the phenotype with an independent reagent and assay.
3. **Therapeutic priority:** tractability, tumour selectivity, safety, and clinical relevance.
4. **Novelty/expectedness:** how much prior literature or model evidence already supports the result.

A biologically reproducible hit may be undruggable, and a low-prior/novel hit may be experimentally strong. One opaque score would hide this distinction and recreate a central weakness of generic target-ranking tools.

### Minimum competitive feature set

- guide-level direction and agreement, number of effective guides, and guide-efficiency proxy;
- replicate, time-point, and context-specific WBC consistency;
- pDNA/initial-library abundance, zero-count fraction, library version, batch, and representation instability;
- raw and CNV/proximity-corrected effects plus change in rank/effect after correction;
- MAGeCK-RRA/MLE, drugZ, Chronos/other compatible callers, and a consensus feature;
- common-essential and pan-effect confounding;
- cell-line expression, copy number, genotype, lineage, and relevant dependency evidence;
- drug target/mechanism/pathway and cross-screen recurrence evidence;
- provenance-stamped literature and database features frozen at the appropriate historical cutoff;
- explicit missingness indicators; lack of an external measurement must not be silently treated as biological absence.

### Labels and selection bias

Use a structured validation event rather than a gene-level binary label:

`screen × gene × drug × cell model × modality × direction × validation reagent × assay × outcome`.

`V2/V3` independently reproduced events can be positives, while technically successful perturbations with failed or opposite phenotypes can be negatives. Technical failures, ambiguous outcomes, and untested genes remain missing/unlabelled. Because investigators preferentially test their strongest or most plausible hits, model:

- `P(selected for testing | observed evidence)`, and
- `P(reproduced | selected and technically valid)`,

or use a carefully justified positive-unlabelled/sensitivity-analysis approach. External validations not derived from the index CRISPR screen are valuable mechanistic priors, but should not automatically become equivalent labels.

### Evaluation needed to exceed the existing literature

- leave-one-source/raw-data-family-out, keeping publication/preprint versions,
  repository mirrors, ORCS records, and alternative methods from one experiment
  together;
- leave-one-study-out;
- leave-one-drug and leave-one-drug-class-out;
- leave-one-cell-line/lineage-out;
- cold-gene or gene-family-aware evaluation;
- temporal split with all literature, ORCS, and database features frozen before the test publication;
- duplicate-screen, shared-raw-source, method-source, and shared-library leakage
  controls.

Report Precision@5/10/20, NDCG, PR-AUC, calibration curves, Brier score/ECE, and uplift over the experimental budget. AUROC alone is insufficient for a top-k selection problem with sparse positives.

Required direct baselines include MAGeCK rank/FDR, drugZ, Chronos FDR where applicable, cross-screen recurrence, simple evidence-weighted/SkywalkR-like ranking, VirtualCRISPR expectedness, regularized logistic regression, and the proposed tree/ranking model. AssayBench should be reported as an adjacent pre-screen benchmark and, only where task-compatible predictions can be produced without leakage, as a non-equivalent exploratory comparator. A prospective case study should freeze the model before examining the held-out screen and pre-specify how many high-, middle-, and discordant-baseline candidates will be tested.

## Bottom line

The landscape is crowded around **hit calling**, **artifact correction**, **virtual screen prediction**, and **generic therapeutic target ranking**. It is much less mature around the proposed supervised endpoint: **whether an already observed, technically characterized drug-response CRISPR hit will reproduce under an independent perturbation and assay**. Building a transparent validation-event registry and evaluating it with strict study/context/time holdouts is therefore the scientific contribution most likely to distinguish CRISPR-EvidenceRank from existing tools.
